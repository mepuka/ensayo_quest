# Research Prompt: Gamification & Gameplay Mechanics for Language Learning

> **Research Brief** — Deep dive into engagement patterns for scenario-based language learning
> Date: 2026-01-18 | Related: `docs/plans/2026-01-18-scenario-ontology-design.md`

## Context

We're building a scenario-based Spanish language learning app (Ensayo Quest) where users speak to accomplish tasks in generated scenarios (e.g., ordering from a restaurant menu). Core mechanics: visual prompts → verbal production → immediate checkoffs → quality scoring. Supports solo and multiplayer modes.

---

## Research Questions

### 1. Engagement Loops in Language Learning Apps

- How do Duolingo, Babbel, and Pimsleur structure their engagement loops? What keeps users coming back daily?
- What's the role of streaks, XP, and leaderboards? When do they help vs. become anxiety-inducing?
- How do successful apps balance "feeling productive" with "actual learning outcomes"?

### 2. Spoken Language Specifically

- What games or apps successfully gamify *speaking* (not just reading/writing/listening)?
- How do apps like Speechling, Elsa Speak, or Glossika handle the awkwardness of speaking aloud?
- What feedback mechanisms work for pronunciation and fluency vs. vocabulary recall?

### 3. Scenario-Based & Immersive Learning

- What research exists on task-based language teaching (TBLT) and its gamification?
- How do immersive games (e.g., language learning through video games) structure progression?
- What's the role of narrative and character in maintaining engagement?

### 4. Visual-to-Verbal Production

- How do flashcard apps (Anki, Memrise) handle image-to-word recall? What works?
- What's the cognitive science behind visual prompts for language production?
- How should difficulty scale—more items? Faster pace? Less visual context?

### 5. Multiplayer & Social Dynamics

- What competitive mechanics work in educational contexts without discouraging weaker learners?
- How do cooperative language learning activities translate to games?
- What's the role of asymmetric play (different roles, different vocab targets)?

### 6. Progression & Mastery Systems

- How should vocabulary "mastery" be modeled? Spaced repetition? Contextual reuse?
- What progression structures create a sense of advancement without artificial gating?
- How do games handle the tension between "review old material" and "learn new material"?

### 7. Feedback & Reward Timing

- What's the optimal delay between action and feedback for learning vs. engagement?
- How do games handle partial success (got the word, but pronunciation was off)?
- What reward schedules (fixed, variable, ratio) work best for language learning?

### 8. Complications & Challenge Design

- How do games introduce "complications" without frustrating players?
- What's the role of failure in language learning games? How is it framed?
- How do adaptive difficulty systems work in educational games?
